# Story structure — any length

Reference for beats, arcs, and hooks. Format files add their own specifics; this is the shared foundation.

## The four elements

Every story needs these. Missing one is why an idea feels flat:

| Element | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Character | "a boy" | "Arjun, 17, who lies about everything" |
| Want | "wants to be happy" | "wants his father to look at him once" |
| Obstacle | "things go wrong" | "his father has already decided he's a failure" |
| Change | "he learns a lesson" | "he tells the truth and loses the lie that protected him" |

If the user gives only a premise, extract the other three *with* them — offer options, don't fill blanks yourself.

## Beat density by length

| Runtime | Units | Beats | Shots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15s | ad | 4 | 3–5 |
| 30s | reel, short | 4–5 | 5–7 |
| 60s | reel, short | 5 | 6–10 |
| 90s | reel, trailer | 5–6 | 10–16 |
| 3 min | short film | 3 acts, 4–6 sequences | 20–35 |
| 10 min | short film | 3 acts, 8–12 sequences | 60–100 |
| 20 min | long episode | teaser + 3–4 acts | 120–200 |

The rule underneath: **one beat per 8–15 seconds** at short lengths, **one sequence per 60–90 seconds** at long lengths.

## Short-form structure (under 2 minutes)

```
hook → setup → turn → payoff → hook out
```

- **Hook** — first 2 seconds. Wrongness or a question, no context.
- **Setup** — who, where, what's wrong. Done by second 15.
- **Turn** — escalate twice. First turn, then a worse turn.
- **Payoff** — the emotional hit.
- **Hook out** — a question, never a moral.

## Long-form structure (over 3 minutes)

**Three acts:**

| Act | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| I | 25% | Establish want and obstacle. Ends on a decision. |
| II | 50% | Escalation and a midpoint flip. Ends at the lowest point. |
| III | 25% | Confrontation and change. |

**Sequences** are the real unit. Each sequence is 1–3 minutes and has its own mini-arc: a want, an obstacle, a turn. A film is 8–12 sequences.

Signpost: if you can't summarise a sequence in one sentence with a turn in it, it's not a sequence — it's padding.

## Hooks

Valid:

- **Interruption** — something is already happening, we're late to it
- **Impossible image** — a visual that raises a question alone
- **Wrong statement** — a line that doesn't fit the scene
- **Direct address** — "I should have known when she said his name."

Invalid: logos, city establishing shots, waking up, title cards, "In a world where".

## Cliffhangers and hooks out

A hook is a **question**, not a cut to black.

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| He looks shocked | shock has no content | He looks at the photo — dated tomorrow |
| To be continued | nothing to wonder about | She's wearing the jacket from episode 1 |
| A door opens | no information | It's the person she buried |

Test: can a viewer state the question in one sentence? If not, rewrite.

## Season and series arcs

- **The question** — one sentence, answered in the finale.
- **Episode function** — each episode changes the answer. If it ends where it started, cut it.
- **Escalation ladder** — obstacle moves closer each time: distant → present → personal → unavoidable.
- **Midpoint flip** — something the audience assumed is false.

4 episodes if the idea is thin. 6 is reliable. 8+ only with 8 real turns.

## Character in a short runtime

You can't develop a character in a minute — you can only **reveal** one.

- Give them a **contradiction**: a cop who steals, a mother who's never home.
- Show it in action, never in dialogue.
- One line of dialogue, maximum. The face does the rest.

## Common failures

1. **Explaining instead of showing** — narration of backstory kills pacing at every length.
2. **Too many characters** — under 90s: two, maximum. Under 3 min: four.
3. **No want** — a story without a want is a mood board.
4. **Uniform pacing** — if every shot is 4s, nothing lands. Vary 2s, 6s, 3s, 5s.
5. **Resolving too early** — save the release for the final 10%.
6. **Continuity drift** — episode 3 quietly changes a detail from episode 1. Track it.
7. **Structure too big for the runtime** — three acts in 60 seconds is a synopsis, not a film.
