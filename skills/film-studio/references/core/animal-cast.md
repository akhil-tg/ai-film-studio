# Anthropomorphic animals

They look like animals — species, fur, muzzle, tail, ears — but they walk upright, speak, wear clothes, use tools and live like people.

## The hard rule

**No humans. Anywhere.**

Not in the background, not in a crowd, not on a poster, in a photo, a reflection, a statue, or a silhouette. Every human role is an animal. A street scene has animal passers-by. A classroom has animal students. A shop has an animal shopkeeper.

Put this in the negatives of every single prompt:

```
No humans, no human faces, no human hands, no people.
```

If one human slips into one frame, the world breaks and the shot is unusable.

## Why it works

- **Instant casting.** Species reads as personality before a line is spoken.
- **Consistency.** An animal design drifts less than a human face across generations.
- **Thumbnail legibility.** A silhouette with ears and a tail survives a 3cm cover image.

## Casting by trait

| Trait | Animal | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Anxious, quick, hunted | hare, deer, mouse | reads as weak — give them one moment of spine |
| Slow, immovable, protective | elephant, bear, rhino | dominates the vertical frame |
| Clever, thieving, surviving | fox, crow, rat | villain-coding if used carelessly |
| Watchful, detached, wise | owl, heron, cat | hard to make warm |
| Loyal, heavy, steady | dog, ox, buffalo | can read as dull |
| Proud, loud, performative | lion, peacock, rooster | comic unless played straight |
| Opportunistic, patient | snake, toad, lizard | mouth shape fights lip sync |
| Innocent, small, expendable | lamb, rabbit, chick | danger reads cheaply |

Rules:

- **One species per character, forever.** Changing it is a recast, not a redesign.
- Don't accidentally pair predator and prey unless the tension is the point.
- Avoid mascot species (panda, penguin, cartoon bear) unless the tone is comic.
- **Scale is composition.** An elephant and a mouse in one frame is a wide shot problem; plan it.

## Anatomy

- Upright, bipedal, roughly human proportions — head about one-seventh of height
- **Hands with fingers and a thumb.** They hold phones and open doors.
- Keep the species head, ears, muzzle, tail, fur pattern
- Human clothing, cut for that body — never a bare animal
- Forward-facing eyes, expressive
- **The mouth must move like a human mouth**, or dialogue won't sync

## Prompt phrasing

```
anthropomorphic hare, upright, human proportions, long ears, grey-brown fur,
wearing a faded school shirt and shorts, speaking with a human mouth.
```

Negatives that matter:

```
No humans, no human hands, no feral animals, no quadruped pose,
no animal muzzle covering the mouth, no mascot costume.
```

## Continuity to lock

Species · fur colour and exact markings · height relative to everyone else · eye colour · clothing · voice.

**Fur patterns drift first.** State the markings twice — in the description and in the negative ("not all-white, not plain").

## Failure modes

1. A human appears in the background
2. The character drops to four legs for a beat
3. Fur markings change between shots
4. The species slips to a cousin — hare becomes rabbit, crow becomes raven
5. The muzzle hides the mouth, so nothing syncs
6. Mascot look: glossy, cartoon, theme-park
7. Scale collapses — a mouse is suddenly waist-high to an elephant
